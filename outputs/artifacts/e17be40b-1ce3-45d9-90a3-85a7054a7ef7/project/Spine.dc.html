You are looking through a slice of every possible beginning of the three-body problem. Each point in the field is a complete starting configuration — three masses, their positions and their velocities — and each has been followed forward in time until its fate is known. The colour records that fate: one body thrown out while the other two pair off, a collision, or three bodies still bound together when time runs out.

The slice is a flat plane through an eight-dimensional space of starting conditions. It can be moved and zoomed like a map, rotated within itself, and tilted to look along any other direction. Where two fates meet, the boundary is not a line: zoom in and it opens into finer and finer structure, points arbitrarily close together ending in entirely different ways.

Point at the field to follow a single beginning, and click to keep it; each kept point is drawn over its place in the slice. The time along the lower edge can be advanced, and the field follows every point further as it goes. Every image carries its own settings, so any view can be recreated exactly from the picture alone.
